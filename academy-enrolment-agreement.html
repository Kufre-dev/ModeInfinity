Enrolment Schedule — Complete Before Acceptance

Complete a separate schedule for each student. The student must receive the applicable course description, fees, timetable, refund and withdrawal terms, assessment requirements and safety policy before committing.

This is a local drafting aid only. It does not save or transmit information. Print the completed page or transfer details to the approved enrolment record. Do not enter unnecessary sensitive personal information.

1. Purpose and Contract Documents

1.1. This Agreement sets out the principal terms for enrolment in the ModeInfinity Shoemaking Academy programme identified in the Enrolment Schedule.

1.2. "ModeInfinity", "we", "us" and "our" mean the Academy operator identified in the completed Enrolment Schedule. "Student" and "you" mean the person enrolled in the programme.

1.3. The contract consists of this Agreement, the completed Enrolment Schedule, the programme description and syllabus, the accepted fee and payment schedule, the published attendance and assessment requirements, the Student Workshop Safety Policy, and any written variation accepted by the parties.

1.4. The Academy must provide material terms before the student commits. An application, enquiry, interview or automated acknowledgement does not by itself guarantee admission or confirm enrolment.

1.5. Enrolment is confirmed when the Academy communicates acceptance through its stated process and the disclosed enrolment conditions have been satisfied.

1.6. Mandatory law prevails over any inconsistent term. Nothing in this Agreement removes a statutory right or legal obligation that cannot lawfully be excluded.

2. Programme Information and Scope

2.1. The Enrolment Schedule must identify the actual programme, its syllabus version, duration, timetable, delivery format, learning outcomes, assessment methods and completion requirements.

2.2. ModeInfinity has discussed the following possible programme pathways. Their current availability, content, fees, duration and admission requirements must be verified before any student is offered a place:

  • Foundations: proposed duration of 3 months.
  • Professional Practice: proposed duration of 6 months.
  • Entrepreneur Mastery: proposed duration of 1–3 years.

2.3. The above descriptions are not a guarantee that every pathway is currently open for enrolment. The accepted programme description and Enrolment Schedule govern the specific student's course.

2.4. The Academy will describe the teaching, practical training, tools, materials, facilities and support included in the programme. Any required purchases, optional services or additional charges must be disclosed before enrolment.

2.5. The Academy may teach shoemaking, footwear design, construction, material selection, finishing, fitting, product development and related business skills where these subjects form part of the accepted syllabus.

2.6. The Academy does not guarantee employment, a specific income, business success, a particular number of customers, external accreditation or professional licensing unless a specific, accurate and substantiated commitment is expressly included in the accepted agreement.

2.7. Any certificate issued must accurately describe the programme completed and identify the issuer. The Academy must not imply government recognition, external accreditation or qualification status unless that status has been verified and may lawfully be claimed.

3. Application, Admission and Enrolment

3.1. Applicants must provide information reasonably required to assess eligibility, contact the applicant, administer enrolment and deliver the programme. Information should be accurate to the best of the applicant's knowledge.

3.2. The Academy will disclose material admission requirements, fees, programme details and conditions before the applicant commits to enrolment.

3.3. A place is not confirmed until the Academy has communicated acceptance and any disclosed conditions for confirmation have been satisfied.

3.4. The Academy should not collect documents or personal information that are unnecessary for admission or programme administration. Personal information will be handled under the Privacy Policy and applicable law.

3.5. Where the programme includes hazardous practical activities, the Academy must establish and communicate the relevant age, supervision, training and safeguarding requirements before the student participates.

3.6. Any material change to the programme offered to a student after acceptance must be communicated clearly, together with the available options and any rights the student may have under the agreement or applicable law.

4. Fees, Payment and Financial Records

4.1. The total programme fee, payment milestones, due dates, accepted payment methods, included materials, excluded costs and any applicable taxes or additional charges must be stated in the Enrolment Schedule or accepted fee schedule.

4.2. The Academy must explain whether tools, leather, soles, adhesives, finishing products, protective equipment, printed materials, assessments, resits or certificates are included in the fee or charged separately.

4.3. Payment methods must be stated in the accepted enrolment documents. Where payment is made by bank transfer or cash, students should use verified Academy payment instructions.

4.4. The Academy will issue a receipt or appropriate transaction record for payments received and maintain relevant financial records in accordance with applicable obligations.

4.5. The Academy will not impose an undisclosed charge merely because an expense arises during the programme. Any additional cost must have been disclosed and accepted, or otherwise be lawfully payable.

4.6. If an instalment is overdue, the Academy should contact the student and explain the amount, due date and available steps to resolve the issue. Any restriction on attendance or access must be proportionate, consistent with the disclosed terms and lawful. It must not be used to avoid a remedy arising from the Academy's own breach.

4.7. The Academy will keep student payment information appropriately restricted. Students must not be asked to disclose passwords, PINs or one-time banking authentication codes.

5. Attendance, Timetable and Participation

5.1. The Academy will provide the student with the applicable timetable, attendance requirements, planned breaks, location and method for communicating schedule changes.

5.2. Students are expected to attend scheduled classes punctually, participate in learning activities and complete assigned practical and theoretical work.

5.3. Students should notify the Academy as soon as reasonably practicable if illness, an emergency or another significant circumstance prevents attendance. The Academy may request reasonable supporting information where necessary, subject to privacy and applicable law.

5.4. The Academy must state whether missed sessions can be made up, whether additional sessions carry a charge, and whether absence affects assessment or completion. These rules must be disclosed before enrolment and applied fairly.

5.5. The Academy should not promise make-up sessions or extensions that it cannot reasonably provide. Where a requested adjustment cannot be accommodated, it should explain the reason and discuss any reasonable alternative.

5.6. Students should not attend practical training while impaired by alcohol, recreational drugs, medication effects that make the activity unsafe, severe fatigue or another condition that creates an immediate safety risk. Any decision to pause participation should be based on reasonable safety grounds and handled respectfully.

5.7. Timetable changes will be communicated as early as reasonably practicable. Material changes affecting duration, location, delivery method or the student's ability to complete the programme must be handled under section 11 and applicable law.

6. Student Responsibilities

6.1. Students must follow reasonable teaching instructions, workshop procedures, equipment restrictions, safety signs and directions from authorized instructors.

6.2. Students must use machinery, cutting tools, stitching equipment, adhesives, solvents and finishing products only when authorized and after receiving the instruction required for the specific activity.

6.3. Students must not deliberately damage, remove or misuse Academy property, equipment, tools, materials, student work or another person's belongings.

6.4. Students should keep their work area reasonably organized, dispose of materials as instructed and report defective equipment, spills, injuries, near misses and unsafe conditions promptly.

6.5. Students must not threaten, harass, discriminate against, intimidate or deliberately disrupt other students, instructors, staff or visitors. Concerns should be raised through the complaint process or directly with the designated Academy contact.

6.6. Students must respect the confidentiality and property rights of the Academy and other students. They should not photograph, record, copy or publish another person's confidential information, design or work without authorization or another lawful basis.

6.7. Students are responsible for asking questions when an instruction, machine operation, material hazard or assessment requirement is unclear. They must not improvise an unsafe procedure to complete an assignment.

6.8. These responsibilities do not remove the Academy's own legal duties to provide appropriate supervision, training, risk controls and a reasonably safe learning environment.

7. Workshop Safety and Practical Training

7.1. Practical shoemaking may involve blades, needles, stitching machinery, grinding or sanding equipment, adhesives, solvents, heat, dust and other hazards. The actual risks depend on the equipment, materials, premises and activities used.

7.2. The Academy will establish and communicate appropriate workshop safety procedures, task-specific training, supervision, equipment restrictions, personal protective equipment requirements, emergency arrangements and incident-reporting procedures.

7.3. Students must not operate restricted machinery or handle hazardous materials without authorization, appropriate instruction and the required protective equipment.

7.4. Students must immediately report a defective guard, damaged cable, unusual machine operation, spill, strong or unusual fumes, injury, near miss or other unsafe condition to the designated instructor or supervisor.

7.5. Anyone who reasonably believes an activity presents immediate danger should stop the activity if it is safe to do so, warn affected people and contact the supervisor. Work should not resume until the concern has been assessed and appropriate controls are in place.

7.6. The Academy will provide relevant safety instructions before practical work begins. A student's acknowledgement of this Agreement or a safety policy is not a waiver of legal rights and does not make the student automatically responsible for every accident.

7.7. Students must follow emergency instructions, keep exits and walkways clear, use protective equipment as directed and avoid unsafe conduct. The Academy must communicate actual emergency arrangements rather than relying on an uncompleted template.

7.8. The Student Workshop Safety Policy forms part of the enrolment documents for practical training. If it has not been completed and implemented for the actual premises and equipment, hazardous practical activities must not begin merely because a student has signed this Agreement.

8. Tools, Materials, Equipment and Student Property

8.1. The Enrolment Schedule or programme materials should identify the tools, equipment, consumables and materials the Academy supplies and those the student must obtain.

8.2. Academy tools and equipment must be used only for authorized learning activities and in accordance with instruction. Students must not remove Academy property from the premises without permission.

8.3. Students should label or otherwise identify personal tools and materials where practical and store them in the designated area. The Academy should explain any available storage arrangements and any limits on responsibility for personal property.

8.4. The Academy will not impose an automatic replacement charge for every reported loss or damage without reviewing the circumstances and identifying a lawful basis. Any charge should be reasonable, supported by evidence and communicated to the student.

8.5. Students must not use another student's tools, materials or work without permission. Disputes over ownership or accidental damage should be reported promptly.

8.6. Any student work retained by the Academy for assessment, display or another purpose should be covered by a clear process stating the purpose, expected period and collection arrangements.

9. Assessment, Progress and Certificates

9.1. The Academy will disclose the learning outcomes, assessment methods, attendance threshold if any, practical standards, grading or pass criteria, resit arrangements and completion requirements for the student's programme.

9.2. Assessments should be based on the disclosed criteria and relevant work. Students should receive reasonable instructions about how and when an assessment will take place.

9.3. If a student does not meet a requirement, the Academy should explain the relevant shortfall and any available reassessment, additional practice or appeal process. Any extra fee must be disclosed and have a lawful basis.

9.4. The Academy may require a student to repeat a practical task where necessary to demonstrate competency or address a safety concern. Any effect on the programme timeline or additional costs must be explained in accordance with the disclosed rules.

9.5. A certificate or completion statement will be issued only when the stated requirements for that credential have been met. The Academy should explain what the certificate represents and whether it is an internal completion certificate or a credential issued or recognized by an external body.

9.6. The Academy will not promise an external qualification, government recognition, employment or commercial success unless the promise is accurate, authorized and expressly documented.

9.7. Students may raise an assessment concern through the Academy's complaint process. The process should identify the grounds for review, who will review it and the expected response period.

10. Withdrawal, Deferral and Refunds

10.1. A student who wishes to withdraw or defer should notify the Academy through the verified enrolment contact and identify the programme, enrolment reference and requested effective date.

10.2. The Academy must provide its actual withdrawal, deferral and refund policy before enrolment. The policy should explain the relevant deadlines, how to submit a request, any eligibility conditions, the treatment of payments already made and the process for communicating a decision.

10.3. This draft does not set a fixed refund percentage or a universal "no refund" rule. The final policy must be based on the Academy's real costs and arrangements and must comply with applicable law. Any deduction must have a lawful basis, be disclosed, be reasonable in context and not operate as an unlawful penalty or double recovery.

10.4. If a student withdraws after a programme has started, the Academy should review the accepted fee schedule, services delivered, materials supplied, payments made and the circumstances of withdrawal. Any amount retained or refunded must be consistent with the agreement and applicable law.

10.5. Where the Academy cancels a programme, cannot provide a material part of the agreed service or materially breaches the enrolment agreement, the student's available remedies will depend on the agreement, the facts and applicable law. This Agreement does not remove a refund or other remedy required by law.

10.6. A deferral request is not automatically approved. The Academy should explain whether a place can be transferred to a later cohort, whether the syllabus or fee has changed, and any reasonable conditions or costs before the student accepts the change.

10.7. If an approved deferral or withdrawal affects materials already ordered or prepared, the Academy should explain the relevant costs and treatment of those materials. It must not automatically assume that every cost can be charged to the student.

10.8. Any agreed or legally required refund should be recorded with the amount, method and processing steps. Provider processing time may affect when funds arrive, but it does not remove an applicable legal deadline.

10.9. Where a student requests a refund because of illness, emergency or another exceptional circumstance, the Academy should review the request fairly under its disclosed policy and applicable law. This clause does not guarantee a refund in every exceptional circumstance.

11. Programme Changes, Cancellation and Interruption

11.1. The Academy will make reasonable efforts to provide the agreed programme in accordance with the accepted schedule and description.

11.2. If the Academy needs to change a class time, instructor, location, material, teaching method or other arrangement, it should communicate the change as early as reasonably practicable. The Academy should consider the effect on learning outcomes, duration, accessibility, cost and the student's ability to complete the programme.

11.3. A material change to the programme must not be concealed in a general timetable notice. The Academy should explain the change and the options available to affected students.

11.4. If the Academy cancels a programme before it starts or cannot provide a material part of it, it should notify affected students and explain the available options, including rescheduling, transfer or refund where appropriate under the agreement and applicable law.

11.5. If an event outside reasonable control interrupts training, the Academy will communicate the known impact and take reasonable steps to reduce disruption. This clause does not automatically excuse avoidable failure, cancel a refund obligation or remove a statutory remedy.

11.6. If the Academy proposes a replacement session or cohort, the student should receive enough information to assess the revised arrangements. Any material variation to the agreement should be documented.

12. Conduct, Discipline and Complaints

12.1. The Academy expects students and staff to treat one another respectfully and to follow reasonable rules for learning, safety, equipment use and protection of property.

12.2. Where a student may have breached a material rule, the Academy should assess the circumstances and, where appropriate, explain the concern and give the student a reasonable opportunity to respond.

12.3. Immediate restriction from a particular machine or activity may be necessary where there is a credible safety risk. Other disciplinary measures should be proportionate, consistently applied and handled fairly.

12.4. Serious or repeated misconduct may lead to suspension or termination of enrolment where the grounds, process and consequences have been clearly disclosed and the action is lawful. Financial consequences must be assessed under the accepted agreement and applicable law, not imposed automatically as punishment.

12.5. Students may submit complaints about teaching, assessment, safety, fees, discrimination, conduct, access arrangements or other Academy matters. Complaints should be acknowledged and reviewed by an appropriate person.

12.6. A complaint should normally identify the issue, relevant dates, people involved where known, supporting information and the outcome sought. The Academy should explain if further information is needed and provide a reasoned response within a reasonable period.

12.7. A student must not be penalized merely for making a good-faith safety report or raising a genuine concern. This does not prevent a fair response to independently established misconduct.

12.8. An internal complaint process does not prevent a student from approaching a competent regulator, tribunal or court where permitted by law.

13. Student Work, Designs and Intellectual Property

13.1. Students retain rights they lawfully own in their original work, subject to third-party rights and any separate written agreement. Enrolment alone does not transfer all student designs, patterns or creative work to the Academy.

13.2. ModeInfinity retains rights it lawfully owns in its name, logos, teaching materials, course notes, templates, patterns, demonstrations, processes and other protected materials, unless a written licence or transfer provides otherwise.

13.3. Students may use Academy materials for the learning purpose for which access is provided. They must not reproduce, sell, publish or commercially exploit protected materials beyond the permitted scope or what the law allows.

13.4. Students should respect confidentiality and the rights of other students when discussing or sharing designs, project work, customer commissions or business information.

13.5. Any commercial collaboration, assignment of intellectual property or licence to use a student's design must be covered by a separate clear agreement stating the permitted use, duration, scope and any payment or attribution terms.

14. Privacy, Photography and Student Information

14.1. The Academy may process personal data reasonably needed to assess applications, administer enrolment, communicate with students, deliver teaching, record attendance, assess progress, maintain financial records, respond to complaints and manage safety.

14.2. The Academy's Privacy Policy should explain what data is collected, why it is processed, the applicable lawful basis, relevant recipients, retention practices, rights and contact route. The notice must match the systems actually used.

14.3. The Academy may need to maintain assessment, attendance, payment or incident records. Access should be restricted to authorized people who need the information for legitimate Academy functions or legal obligations.

14.4. Photography or video used for safety, internal documentation, assessment and public marketing can involve different purposes. The Academy should provide appropriate notice and obtain separate permission where required before using an identifiable student's image, voice, testimonial or work in promotional content.

14.5. Refusing optional publicity permission should not by itself prevent enrolment. Any withdrawal of publicity consent will be handled in accordance with applicable law and the circumstances of material already lawfully produced or distributed.

14.6. Students should not record or publish another student's personal information, image, voice or identifiable work without appropriate authorization or another lawful basis.

14.7. Where the programme involves a minor, the Academy must establish the required age, guardian authorization, safeguarding, privacy and workshop supervision procedures before enrolment and practical activity.

14.8. Students may contact officialmodeinfinity11@gmail.com about a privacy concern or data request, subject to the verified privacy contact arrangements published by ModeInfinity.

15. Accessibility, Support and Adjustments

15.1. Applicants and students may contact the Academy to discuss access needs or adjustments relevant to learning and practical training.

15.2. The Academy will consider requests fairly and in accordance with applicable law, the nature of the activity, available facilities and reasonable safety requirements. Where an adjustment cannot be provided as requested, the Academy should explain why and discuss possible alternatives where appropriate.

15.3. Information about a student's health, disability or support needs should be requested only where reasonably necessary and handled with appropriate privacy safeguards.

15.4. No statement in this Agreement guarantees that every adjustment will be available in every circumstance. Equally, the Academy must not use a general clause to avoid duties imposed by applicable law.

16. Responsibilities and Legal Rights

16.1. The Academy will take reasonable steps to deliver the agreed programme, communicate material requirements, provide relevant instruction and implement appropriate safety arrangements for the actual training environment.

16.2. Students must follow reasonable instructions, disclose relevant information when properly requested, use equipment only as authorized and report hazards or incidents promptly.

16.3. Responsibility for an injury, loss, damage or other event depends on the facts, applicable law, the conduct of the parties and the relevant risk controls. Signing this Agreement does not make a student automatically responsible for every event.

16.4. Nothing in this Agreement excludes or restricts liability where such exclusion or restriction is prohibited by law. Any limitation permitted by law must be clear, reasonable and proportionate, and must not remove a statutory remedy.

16.5. The Academy will not require a student to indemnify the Academy for the Academy's own unlawful conduct or for liability that cannot lawfully be transferred.

16.6. The student remains responsible for personal decisions and conduct within the limits of applicable law. This provision does not replace the Academy's own obligations.

17. General Provisions

17.1. Communications

Material communications about fees, attendance, assessment, deferral, withdrawal, complaints or programme changes should be recorded through the official contact method identified in the Enrolment Schedule. Students should retain copies of important messages and receipts.

17.2. Changes to this Agreement

The Academy may update this Agreement for future enrolments and will identify the effective date. It will not apply a material adverse change retroactively to an existing student without a lawful basis and any agreement required by law.

17.3. No automatic waiver

A delay in enforcing a term does not automatically waive a right. Any waiver or variation should be recorded clearly and must be lawful.

17.4. Severability

If a provision is found unenforceable, the remaining provisions continue to apply to the extent permitted by law. This does not authorize removal of a student's mandatory legal rights.

17.5. Governing law

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, subject to mandatory applicable law and the jurisdiction of competent courts, tribunals and regulators.

19. Enrolment Readiness Checklist

Complete this checklist before using the Agreement for real enrolments. Do not publish internal checklist content on the public website.

  • Verify the Academy's legal operator, CAC details, authorized representative and current training address.
  • Confirm which programmes are currently available and approve the syllabus, duration, timetable and admission criteria for each.
  • Approve the complete fee schedule, payment milestones, included materials, optional purchases and any additional charges.
  • Approve the actual withdrawal, deferral, cancellation and refund policy with Nigerian counsel.
  • Define attendance thresholds, missed-session rules, make-up classes, extensions, resits and assessment appeals.
  • Confirm certificate wording and verify any claimed external accreditation or recognition.
  • Complete a site-specific risk assessment for the actual machines, tools, adhesives, solvents and workshop activities.
  • Complete and implement the Student Workshop Safety Policy before hazardous practical training begins.
  • Verify equipment training, supervision, PPE, ventilation, emergency routes, first aid, fire arrangements and incident reporting.
  • Establish a process for handling student work, tools, storage, damage reports and collection of retained projects.
  • Define how student designs and intellectual property will be handled and obtain separate agreements for commercial use where required.
  • Audit forms, email, hosting, messaging and other services that process student personal data.
  • Review privacy notices, retention, access controls, publicity permission and any recording or photography process.
  • Establish age and guardian-verification rules before accepting minors into any programme or hazardous activity.
  • Publish a complaint contact and define who receives, investigates and responds to complaints.
  • Ensure all documents are supplied before enrolment and that acceptance can be evidenced.
  • Obtain qualified Nigerian legal review and record the approved version and effective date.
  • Test the final enrolment workflow on desktop and mobile before making it available to applicants.
  • Remove unresolved placeholders and internal notes from the public version only after verification and approval.

Publication control: Keep the draft non-indexable and do not use it to obtain student acceptance until programme details, financial terms, safety procedures, privacy arrangements and legal review are complete.
