Student Workshop Safety Policy

Safety expectations for students, instructors, workshop supervisors and authorized visitors participating in ModeInfinity shoemaking training and practical workshop activities.

Important safety limitation

This is a policy framework, not a completed workshop risk assessment. It does not authorize students to use machinery, blades, adhesives, solvents, grinders or other potentially hazardous equipment without appropriate assessment, instruction, supervision and authorization. Management must tailor it to the actual premises, equipment, materials, age and experience of students, and applicable Nigerian requirements.

1. Purpose and Scope

ModeInfinity aims to provide a workshop environment in which students can learn shoemaking skills while identifying, preventing and responding appropriately to foreseeable risks.

This policy applies to:

  • Students enrolled in ModeInfinity shoemaking programmes.
  • Instructors, technicians, workshop supervisors and academy management.
  • Authorized contractors and visitors entering practical work areas.
  • Practical activities involving footwear design, cutting, stitching, lasting, sole preparation, finishing, adhesives and related processes.

This policy applies whenever academy workshop activities are conducted, including approved demonstrations and supervised practical sessions. It must be read with equipment-specific instructions, chemical safety information, risk assessments, emergency procedures and any applicable premises rules.

2. Core Safety Principles

Everyone in the workshop must follow these principles:

  • Safety takes priority over speed, production targets and completing a project.
  • No person may operate equipment without the required training and authorization.
  • Hazards, damaged equipment, unsafe conditions and near misses must be reported promptly.
  • Safety guards, interlocks, ventilation and other protective measures must not be bypassed.
  • Only approved tools, materials and methods may be used for each task.
  • Instructions from the designated workshop supervisor must be followed.
  • Any person may stop an activity they reasonably believe presents an immediate danger.
Stop-work rule

If an activity appears unsafe, stop when it is safe to do so, keep others clear of the hazard, and notify the workshop supervisor. Do not restart until the hazard has been assessed and the supervisor has authorized the activity to resume.

3. Roles and Responsibilities

3.1 Academy Management

  • Provide a suitable workshop, appropriate equipment and adequate safety arrangements.
  • Ensure documented risk assessments are completed and reviewed for actual workshop tasks.
  • Appoint competent persons to supervise practical activities.
  • Arrange suitable training, protective equipment, first-aid provisions and emergency procedures.
  • Maintain relevant equipment, inspection, training and incident records.
  • Investigate reported incidents and ensure corrective actions are tracked.

3.2 Instructors and Workshop Supervisors

  • Explain task-specific hazards and demonstrate safe methods before work begins.
  • Confirm that students are trained and authorized for the equipment they use.
  • Check the work area and equipment before practical sessions.
  • Ensure guards and protective systems remain in place and functional.
  • Provide supervision appropriate to the task, the student's experience and the level of risk.
  • Stop unsafe work and escalate hazards that cannot be corrected immediately.

3.3 Students

  • Attend required safety induction and task-specific instruction.
  • Follow approved procedures and use required protective equipment correctly.
  • Use only equipment for which they have been authorized.
  • Keep work areas orderly and report injuries, near misses, damaged tools and unsafe conditions.
  • Never interfere with guards, electrical controls, extraction systems or safety devices.
  • Stop and seek assistance when uncertain about a tool, material or process.

3.4 Contractors and Visitors

Contractors and visitors must receive appropriate site instructions, remain in authorized areas, follow the supervisor's directions and use any protective equipment required for their visit. Visitors must not handle tools or machinery unless separately authorized.

4. Safety Induction and Equipment Authorization

Before participating in practical work, each student must complete the induction requirements established by the academy.

The induction process must cover, at minimum:

  • Workshop layout, restricted areas, exits and assembly arrangements.
  • How to report hazards, injuries, near misses and equipment faults.
  • First-aid arrangements and the location of available safety equipment.
  • Required clothing and task-specific personal protective equipment.
  • Safe handling, carrying, storage and disposal of tools and materials.
  • Fire precautions and the procedure for raising an alarm and evacuating.
  • Relevant hazards identified in the workshop's risk assessments.

Induction alone does not authorize the use of every tool or machine. Before independent use of any equipment, a student must receive equipment-specific instruction, demonstrate competence where required, and obtain authorization from the designated instructor or supervisor.

Training and authorization records must identify the student, equipment or activity, instructor, date, and any restrictions or refresher-training requirements.

5. Clothing and Personal Protective Equipment

Clothing and protective equipment must be selected according to the hazards of the specific task and the findings of the relevant risk assessment. Management must document the required equipment for each approved activity.

5.1 General Workshop Clothing

  • Wear clothing that allows safe movement without loose material that could become caught in machinery.
  • Secure long hair and keep dangling jewelry, lanyards and accessories away from moving equipment.
  • Wear suitable closed footwear appropriate to the work area and task.
  • Keep personal belongings outside work areas where they could create obstructions or hazards.

5.2 Task-Specific Protection

Depending on the task and assessed risk, protective equipment may include eye protection, hearing protection, suitable gloves, respiratory protection or other specialist equipment. These examples are not a universal specification for every task.

The academy must identify the correct type, fit, limitations, inspection, cleaning, storage and replacement arrangements for each required item. Respiratory protection must not be treated as a substitute for effective ventilation or source control.

Glove and machine compatibility

Gloves can introduce an entanglement hazard around rotating or moving machinery. Do not wear gloves near such equipment unless a documented assessment and the equipment's safe operating instructions expressly permit them.

6. Hand Tools, Blades, Awls and Needles

Shoemaking tools may create risks of cuts, punctures, impact injuries and damage to nearby persons or materials.

  • Use the correct tool for the job and inspect it before use.
  • Keep blades and sharp points controlled, directed away from the body and other people.
  • Cut on a suitable, stable cutting surface; never use a hand or leg as a cutting support.
  • Carry and pass sharp tools in a controlled manner, with the point or cutting edge safely directed.
  • Use protective covers or designated storage when tools are not in use.
  • Do not leave blades, awls, needles or sharp offcuts loose on benches or floors.
  • Remove damaged tools from service and report them to the supervisor.
  • Dispose of used blades and other sharps in an approved, puncture-resistant container.
  • Do not improvise repairs or modify tools unless an authorized person has approved the work.

Students must receive instruction in the safe method for each cutting, punching, trimming and stitching operation. The supervisor must determine when direct supervision is necessary.

7. Stitching Machines and Powered Equipment

Powered stitching machines and other equipment can cause puncture, entanglement, crushing, abrasion, electrical, noise and projectile hazards. The actual equipment inventory must be assessed before this section is adopted.

7.1 Before Operation

  • Confirm that the student is authorized to use the particular machine.
  • Check that guards, covers, controls, lighting and other safety devices are present and functional.
  • Check the work area for loose items, obstruction and unsuitable materials.
  • Understand the machine's operating instructions, safe limits and stopping procedure.
  • Report any unusual noise, vibration, overheating, damage or malfunction before use.

7.2 During Operation

  • Keep fingers and other body parts clear of needles, blades, pinch points and moving components.
  • Do not distract the operator or reach across moving equipment.
  • Do not remove, defeat or bypass guards or safety devices.
  • Do not leave running equipment unattended unless the approved procedure expressly permits it.
  • Keep loose clothing, hair, jewelry and unsuitable accessories away from moving parts.
  • Use the machine only for its intended purpose and within the manufacturer's instructions.

7.3 Adjustments, Jams and Maintenance

Stop the machine before making adjustments, changing needles, clearing jams, cleaning or carrying out other work that could expose a person to moving parts. Where hazardous energy could cause injury, the equipment must be isolated and secured using the approved procedure before the work begins.

Repairs and electrical maintenance must be carried out only by appropriately competent and authorized persons. Students must not undertake maintenance beyond the tasks they have been trained and authorized to perform.

The academy must create machine-specific operating instructions for every powered device in use. This policy does not replace those instructions.

8. Adhesives, Solvents, Finishing Products and Chemicals

Footwear production may involve adhesives, cleaners, dyes, coatings, finishing agents and other products that can present inhalation, skin-contact, eye-contact, fire or environmental hazards. The hazards vary by product.

  • Use only approved products and follow their labels, safety data sheets and documented handling instructions.
  • Review the risks before introducing a new chemical or changing a process.
  • Use products only in the designated area with the ventilation and controls required by the assessment.
  • Keep containers clearly labeled and closed when not in use.
  • Never transfer chemicals into food or drink containers.
  • Keep incompatible products apart and follow approved storage requirements.
  • Keep ignition sources away from flammable products and their vapors.
  • Use only the protective equipment specified for the actual product and task.
  • Do not eat, drink or store food in chemical handling areas.
  • Report spills, leaks, unusual odors, exposure and damaged containers immediately.

Management must determine whether local exhaust ventilation, specialized storage, spill-control materials, fire precautions or other controls are required. Students must not clean up a chemical spill unless the academy's procedure specifically authorizes them and they are trained and equipped to do so.

Chemical waste, contaminated materials and empty containers must be handled and disposed of according to the product instructions and applicable requirements. Do not pour chemicals into drains unless an approved procedure expressly permits it.

9. Housekeeping, Materials and Storage

A clean and organized workshop reduces trips, falls, cuts, fire risks and damage to materials or equipment.

  • Keep walkways, exits, electrical panels and emergency equipment accessible.
  • Store leather, soles, tools, patterns and supplies in designated areas.
  • Keep materials stable and prevent stacks from falling or obstructing access.
  • Clean spills promptly using the approved method for the substance involved.
  • Remove scraps, offcuts, thread, packaging and waste from work surfaces and walkways.
  • Keep sharp waste separate and place it in suitable designated containers.
  • Return tools and equipment to their designated storage locations after use.
  • Do not block ventilation openings or cover equipment vents.
  • Report damaged storage racks, unstable stacks, leaks or recurring housekeeping problems.

The instructor or designated person must establish and communicate responsibility for cleaning at the end of each practical session.

10. Electrical Safety

  • Use electrical equipment only when it is suitable for the environment and task.
  • Check for visible damage to cables, plugs, switches and equipment before use.
  • Keep electrical equipment and connections away from liquids and wet areas.
  • Do not use damaged equipment, exposed wiring, improvised connections or overloaded outlets.
  • Do not pull a plug out by its cable.
  • Report electrical faults and remove affected equipment from use where it is safe to do so.
  • Do not attempt electrical repairs unless properly competent and authorized.

The academy must establish inspection, maintenance, isolation and repair arrangements appropriate to its equipment and premises. Any suspected electrical emergency must be handled under the site's emergency procedure; do not touch a person who may still be in contact with a live electrical source.

11. Fire Prevention and Emergency Response

11.1 Prevention

  • Keep escape routes and exits clear and accessible.
  • Store flammable products according to their safety information and the site's approved arrangements.
  • Control ignition sources where flammable materials or vapors may be present.
  • Report damaged electrical equipment, overheating, smoke, leaks and other warning signs.
  • Do not obstruct fire alarms, extinguishers or other emergency equipment.

11.2 When an Emergency Occurs

  1. Stop work if it is safe to do so.
  2. Raise the alarm using the site's established method.
  3. Warn people in immediate danger without putting yourself at risk.
  4. Evacuate by the designated route and follow the instructions of the responsible person.
  5. Go to the confirmed assembly point and remain available for a headcount.
  6. Do not re-enter until the responsible emergency authority or designated site lead gives permission.

The academy must select and maintain appropriate fire equipment and train designated personnel based on the actual site risks.

12. First Aid, Injuries and Incident Reporting

All injuries, exposures, equipment failures, unsafe conditions and near misses must be reported to the workshop supervisor as soon as practicable. A near miss must be reported even when no injury occurred.

12.1 Immediate Response

  • Stop the activity and prevent others from entering the hazard area.
  • Raise the alarm or request assistance where necessary.
  • Arrange help from a designated first aider or emergency service, as appropriate.
  • Do not provide treatment beyond your training and authorization.
  • Do not move a seriously injured person unless there is an immediate danger requiring it.
  • Preserve the incident area when safe and appropriate, except where action is needed to protect life or prevent further harm.

12.3 Investigation and Follow-Up

Management or the designated competent person must review reported incidents, identify contributing factors, decide on corrective actions, assign responsibility and deadlines, and record when actions are completed. Where necessary, the affected activity must remain suspended until the risk has been reassessed and adequate controls are in place.

Incident records must be stored securely, access restricted to authorized persons, and retained according to the academy's approved recordkeeping requirements.

13. Student Conduct and Stop-Work Authority

Students must not:

  • Use tools, machinery or chemicals without required authorization.
  • Run, play-fight, distract operators or engage in horseplay in practical areas.
  • Use equipment while impaired, excessively fatigued or unable to work safely.
  • Bring unauthorized tools, chemicals, ignition sources or other hazardous items into the workshop.
  • Remove guards or safety devices or interfere with another person's safe operation.
  • Ignore safety instructions or conceal an injury, spill, fault or near miss.
  • Allow unauthorized persons to operate equipment or enter restricted areas.

Students should ask for clarification whenever they do not understand an instruction or believe a method is unsafe. A student who raises a genuine safety concern must be heard and the concern assessed.

Where a safety rule is breached, the instructor or management may stop the activity, withdraw equipment authorization, require additional training, or apply the academy's established disciplinary process. Responses should be proportionate, documented where appropriate, and consistent with the student's enrolment terms and applicable law.

14. Safety Records, Monitoring and Review

Management must maintain records appropriate to the workshop, including:

  • Workshop and task-specific risk assessments.
  • Equipment inventory, operating instructions, inspection and maintenance records.
  • Student induction, training, competence and authorization records.
  • Protective equipment requirements and relevant issue or inspection records.
  • Safety inspections, hazard reports, incident and near-miss reports, and corrective actions.
  • Emergency arrangements, drills where applicable, and training records.
  • Relevant chemical inventories and safety data sheets.

Records containing personal information must be handled under the academy's applicable privacy and recordkeeping procedures.

This policy must be reviewed:

  • Before the workshop first operates under this policy.
  • When premises, equipment, chemicals or processes change materially.
  • Following an incident, near miss, inspection finding or identified control failure.
  • When legal requirements or relevant manufacturer instructions change.
  • At the interval approved by management and recorded in the policy register.

The policy owner must record the review outcome, approved amendments, responsible approver and next review date. Staff and students must receive updates relevant to their work.

15. Management Completion Checklist

The items below are internal implementation controls. Complete and verify them before issuing the policy. A written policy alone does not establish that the workshop is safe.

  • Confirm the operating address and actual workshop layout.
  • Inventory all tools, machines, chemicals and other hazardous materials.
  • Complete and approve task-specific risk assessments.
  • Document equipment-specific operating and isolation procedures.
  • Confirm required PPE and its suitability for each task.
  • Confirm ventilation, fire precautions and chemical storage arrangements.
  • Verify first-aid arrangements, emergency contacts, exits and assembly point.
  • Identify competent instructors, first aiders and emergency responsibilities.
  • Establish induction, training, authorization and refresher arrangements.
  • Establish incident reporting, investigation and corrective-action procedures.
  • Obtain management approval and qualified Nigerian legal/safety review as appropriate.
  • Remove draft markers only after all material details have been verified and approved.

17. Policy Approval

This policy becomes operational only after the authorized approver confirms that the policy and the associated site-specific safety arrangements are complete.
